Part one of five

Infinite Software

Machine labor makes software unlimited. This part establishes the era: why the cost of producing software has collapsed by roughly four thousand times in a single step, and why that collapse moves the money from making software to running it.

1.1Software production just broke free of human hands

For three decades the cost of software work fell in steps, and each step took about a decade to arrive. Artisan engineering ran near two hundred dollars a unit of work. Offshoring and outsourcing took it to sixty. Open source and reuse took it to twenty. Copilots took it to five.

Then, in a single step, agent production took it to five cents.

The magnitude matters more than the direction. When any input becomes four thousand times cheaper, nobody buys the same quantity of it more cheaply — they buy vastly more of it. That is the whole setup for the inversion in §1.3: if making software approaches free, the software population explodes, and everything that software must consume in order to run becomes the scarce good.

1.2Labor is no longer human-limited

The price collapse is the symptom. The regime change is the cause. A copilot keeps a person inside every loop: you prompt, it answers, you check, you prompt again. An agent takes an objective and runs — planning, acting, verifying, looping until the work is done. The human stops being the operator and becomes the manager.

Three consequences follow, and they compound. The human bottleneck disappears, so attention-hours no longer cap output. Work parallelizes, because agents launch agents — ten to a hundred simultaneous workstreams. And the duty cycle changes: forty attention-hours a week becomes one hundred and sixty-eight machine-hours.
